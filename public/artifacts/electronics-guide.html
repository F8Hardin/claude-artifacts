Section 01

What is Electricity?

Everything is made of atoms. At the center is a nucleus (protons + neutrons). Orbiting it are electrons — tiny, negatively charged particles. Electricity is simply the movement of electrons from one place to another.

Simplified atom — electrons orbit the nucleus

Conductors vs. Insulators

Conductor 🔌

Electrons can move freely. Examples: copper wire, aluminum, gold. This is why wires are copper inside.

Insulator 🛡️

Electrons are tightly bound. Examples: rubber, plastic, glass, air. This is why wires have a rubber coat.

Section 02

Ohm's Law — The Golden Rule

Georg Ohm discovered that voltage, current, and resistance are tied together by a beautifully simple equation. If you know two of the three, you always know the third.

V = I × R Voltage = Current × Resistance

Rearranged:   I = V ÷ R   and   R = V ÷ I

💡

Example: A 9V battery pushes current through a 470Ω resistor. How much current flows?
I = 9 ÷ 470 = 0.019 A (19 milliamps) — just enough to glow an LED safely.

Power Formula

P = V × I Power (Watts) = Voltage × Current  |  Also: P = I² × R  |  P = V² ÷ R

Section 03

Batteries — Stored Chemical Energy

A battery doesn't store electricity directly — it stores chemical energy and converts it to electrical energy on demand through a chemical reaction. A simple battery has three parts: an anode (negative terminal), a cathode (positive terminal), and an electrolyte (the medium that lets ions travel).

Basic voltaic cell / battery structure

How to Build a Lemon Battery (Real Experiment)

  • Grab a fresh lemon and roll it on a table to soften it (releases juice inside).
  • Push a zinc nail or galvanized screw into one side — this is your anode (−).
  • Push a copper coin or copper wire into the other side — this is your cathode (+).
  • The lemon juice acts as the electrolyte, allowing ions to pass between the metals.
  • Connect a small LED between the two metal pieces. It should glow faintly (~0.9V per lemon). Chain 3–4 lemons in series for more voltage!

Battery Capacity — How Much Energy Can It Store?

Battery capacity is measured in milliamp-hours (mAh) or amp-hours (Ah). A 2000 mAh battery can supply 2000 mA (2A) for 1 hour, or 200 mA for 10 hours.

Energy (Wh) = Capacity (Ah) × Voltage (V) Example: 2 Ah × 3.7 V = 7.4 Watt-hours stored
📱

Real world: A typical iPhone battery is ~3,279 mAh at 3.85V = ≈12.6 Wh. A Tesla Model 3 Long Range stores ~82,000 Wh (82 kWh) — about 6,500× more.

Series vs. Parallel Battery Connections

Series (adds voltage) ⚡

Stack two 1.5V AA batteries end-to-end → 3V total, same capacity. Used in TV remotes, flashlights.

V_total = V₁ + V₂ + ...
Parallel (adds capacity) 🔋

Connect two batteries side-by-side → same voltage, double the capacity. Used in power banks, EVs.

Ah_total = Ah₁ + Ah₂ + ...
Section 04

How Magnets Generate Electricity

Michael Faraday discovered in 1831 that moving a magnet through a coil of wire creates a voltage. This is called electromagnetic induction — and it's how virtually all large-scale electricity is generated on Earth.

Faraday's Law — moving magnet induces current in coil

Faraday's Law (simplified)

EMF = −N × (ΔΦ / Δt) EMF = induced voltage  |  N = number of coil turns  |  ΔΦ/Δt = how fast magnetic field changes

In plain English: More coil turns + faster-moving magnet = higher voltage. This is why generators spin rapidly.

From Magnet to Power Grid

  • Energy source (steam from coal/nuclear, falling water, wind) spins a turbine.
  • The turbine rotates an electromagnet inside a huge coil assembly — the generator.
  • This produces AC (alternating current) — voltage that cycles back and forth at 60 Hz (US) or 50 Hz (Europe).
  • A transformer steps voltage up to ~500,000V for efficient long-distance transmission.
  • Local transformers step voltage back down to 120V/240V for your home.
🌊

AC vs DC: Batteries produce DC (direct current) — electrons always flow one direction. Generators produce AC — electrons alternate direction. Your phone's charger converts AC from the wall to DC for the battery.

Section 05

Capacitors — Fast Energy Storage

A capacitor stores energy in an electric field between two conductive plates separated by an insulator (called a dielectric). Unlike batteries, they charge and discharge almost instantly — but hold far less total energy.

Capacitor — charges stored on two parallel plates

Capacitance Formula

Q = C × V Charge (Coulombs) = Capacitance (Farads) × Voltage (Volts)
Energy = ½ × C × V² Energy stored in Joules  |  C in Farads  |  V in Volts
⚡

Example: A 1000µF (0.001F) capacitor charged to 9V stores:   E = ½ × 0.001 × 9² = 0.0405 Joules. Tiny — but released in microseconds, the power can be enormous. Camera flashes use this!

Section 06

Basic Wiring & Circuit Concepts

A circuit is a closed loop that electrons can travel through. Break the loop anywhere and current stops flowing — that's how a switch works.

Simple series circuit: battery + resistor + LED + switch

Series vs. Parallel Circuits

Series Circuit

Components in a single chain. Same current flows through all. If one breaks — everything goes dark. Old Christmas lights were like this.

R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃
Parallel Circuit

Components in separate branches. Each gets full voltage. One fails, others keep working. Home wiring uses this.

1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂

Section 07

Overloading a Circuit — Danger & Protection

Every wire has a maximum current rating. Push more current than it can handle and the wire heats up — in extreme cases, hot enough to start a fire. This is why circuit protection exists.

🔥

A typical household extension cord is rated for 15A. Plugging in a 1500W heater (12.5A) + a 1200W microwave (10A) = 22.5A total — well above the limit. The cord will overheat.

Power Draw Formula

I = P ÷ V Current (A) = Power (W) ÷ Voltage (V)  |  In the US: 120V standard outlets

Protection Devices

⚡ Fuse

A thin wire that melts and breaks the circuit when current exceeds its rating. One-time use. Rated in amps (e.g. 5A, 15A, 30A). Found in cars, older home panels.

🔘 Circuit Breaker

An electromagnetic switch that trips open when overloaded. Reusable — just reset it. All modern homes use a breaker panel. Each circuit in your home has a 15A or 20A breaker.

🔌 GFCI Outlet

A Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter detects current leaking through an unintended path (like you). Trips in 1/30th of a second. Required near water — bathrooms, kitchens, outdoors.

Safe Load Calculation

Max safe load (W) = Breaker rating (A) × Voltage (V) × 0.8 The 0.8 factor is the NEC 80% rule — never run a circuit above 80% capacity continuously
🏠

Example: A 20A / 120V circuit → max load = 20 × 120 × 0.8 = 1,920 Watts. Don't plug more than that into one circuit.

Section 08

Beginner Projects

PROJECT 01

Capacitor Flash — Charge & Release an LED

What you need: 9V battery, 1000µF capacitor, 470Ω resistor, LED, push button, breadboard, jumper wires.

How it works: The capacitor charges through the resistor when connected to the battery. When you press the button, it discharges instantly through the LED causing a bright, brief flash. You'll see the LED slowly dim as the capacitor drains — this is an RC decay curve.

  • Connect (+) battery to one leg of the 470Ω resistor.
  • Connect other leg of resistor to (+) pin of capacitor.
  • Connect (−) battery to (−) capacitor pin (capacitor is now charging).
  • Add a push button in series with the LED across the capacitor terminals.
  • Press button — watch the flash and observe the glow fade over ~1 second.
Time constant τ = R × C = 470 × 0.001 = 0.47 seconds The capacitor reaches ~63% charge after one τ — and ~99% after 5τ (2.35 seconds)
PROJECT 02

Voltage Divider — Two Resistors, Adjustable Output

What you need: 9V battery, two resistors (any values), voltmeter.

How it works: Two resistors in series split the voltage proportionally. This is how volume knobs, sensors, and microcontroller input pins work.

V_out = V_in × R₂ / (R₁ + R₂) With 9V, R₁=1kΩ, R₂=2kΩ → V_out = 9 × 2/(1+2) = 6V
PROJECT 03

Hand-Crank Generator — Turn Motion into Light

What you need: Small DC motor (from a toy), LED, two wires.

How it works: A DC motor run backwards is a generator. Spin the motor shaft by hand — the moving magnets inside induce a current. Connect an LED and watch it light up. Spin faster = brighter light.

This demonstrates Faraday's law directly. You can measure the output with a voltmeter and observe how RPM directly affects voltage.

PROJECT 04

Fuse Demonstration — Purposeful Overload

What you need: 9V battery, a thin strand of steel wool (0000 grade), wires. Do this outdoors.

How it works: Touch both terminals of a 9V battery to a single strand of fine steel wool. The high current through the thin wire overheats it, causing it to glow red and burn — exactly what a fuse does in a controlled way. This illustrates P = I²R heating.

⚠️

Have water nearby. Keep away from flammables. This is why fuses and circuit breakers are so critical.

Section 09

Real-World Systems

ELECTRIC VEHICLES

Tesla Model 3 — Parallel Battery Packs

The Model 3 Long Range contains ~4,400 cylindrical lithium-ion cells (21700 format) arranged in modules, which combine series (for voltage) and parallel (for capacity) connections. The total pack delivers 82 kWh at ~350–400V. A BMS (Battery Management System) monitors every cell's temperature and voltage — preventing overcharge, overdischarge, and thermal runaway. The same Ohm's Law and series/parallel rules you just learned govern a $50,000 battery system.

POWER GRID

Hoover Dam — Electromagnetic Induction at Scale

Hoover Dam's 17 generators each contain a rotor with electromagnets spinning inside a massive coil stator. The same principle as your hand-crank motor — except each generator rotor weighs 4.5 million lbs and spins at 180 RPM. The dam produces up to 2,080 megawatts — enough for ~1.3 million homes. Output transformers step voltage from ~16,500V up to 287,000V for transmission.

CONSUMER ELECTRONICS

Camera Flash — Capacitor Discharge

A DSLR or strobe flash uses a large capacitor (often 300–1000µF at 300V+) charged by a small DC-DC converter. When you shoot, the capacitor dumps ~1–10 Joules through a xenon tube in microseconds — producing a burst of light that no battery could deliver at that speed. The "recycling time" you wait between flashes is the capacitor recharging from the battery. Same physics as Project 01, scaled up.

HOME WIRING

Your House Panel — Parallel Circuits + Breakers

Every outlet, light, and appliance in your home runs on a parallel circuit branching from your main panel. This means each device gets full 120V (or 240V for dryers/ovens) regardless of what else is on. Each branch circuit has a circuit breaker — a resettable fuse — typically rated 15A or 20A for outlets, 30–50A for large appliances. The panel itself has a main breaker (100A–200A) protecting the whole house. The same Ohm's Law calculation from Section 2 is used by electricians every day.

RENEWABLE ENERGY

Solar + Grid-Scale Storage

Solar panels generate DC electricity directly from photons displacing electrons (the photoelectric effect). An inverter converts this DC to AC for grid compatibility. Grid-scale battery facilities (like Moss Landing in California — 3,000 MWh) use thousands of lithium-ion battery modules in series-parallel arrangements, managed by industrial BMS systems, to store solar energy and release it at night. The Faraday, Ohm, and capacitance principles you've learned here underlie every watt of it.
